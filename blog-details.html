The Art of Layering Fragrances

By Isabelle Dupont • August 15, 2025

Fragrance layering, also known as scent cocktails, is the ancient Middle Eastern tradition of mixing multiple perfumes to create a bespoke scent that is unique to the wearer. It is an art form that allows you to express your mood, personality, and style in a way that a single perfume often cannot.

The key to successful layering is understanding the notes. You don't necessarily spray two perfumes on top of each other. Instead, apply a heavier scent first—typically woods, musks, or oriental notes—and top it off with a lighter one, such as citrus or florals. This prevents the lighter notes from being overpowered.

Rules of Engagement

1. Moisturize First: Scent lasts longer on hydrated skin. Use an unscented lotion or a matching body cream.
2. Heavy to Light: Always apply the strongest scent first.
3. Don't Rub: Rubbing your wrists together breaks down the molecules and ruins the top notes.

"Layering is not about masking a scent, but enhancing it. It is about creating a harmony where 1 + 1 equals 3."

Experiment with contrasting scents. A vanilla base can sweeten a spicy oud, while a citrus top note can brighten a heavy floral. The possibilities are endless, and the result is a signature trail that is yours alone.
